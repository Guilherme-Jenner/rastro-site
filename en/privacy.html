Privacy Policy

Version 2026-11 · updated on 2026-11-12 · Português

In short: Rastro has no account and no server of its own, and it does not receive what you write. Habits, journal, reminders, photos and videos stay on your device. If you turn on iCloud sync, they go to your iCloud, which we cannot access. The only communication with a third-party service outside the system is the Pro subscription, which carries none of your content.

1. Who we are

Rastro is a habits, journal and reminders app for iPhone and Android, developed by Guilherme Jenner. To talk about personal data, use the contact in section 13.

2. What Rastro collects

We do not collect the app's content. There is no sign-up, no login and no Rastro server that receives your habits, journal, reminders, photos or videos. We do not use analytics, advertising or tracking tools, and we do not sell or share data. The only exception is the Pro subscription, explained in section 8: it involves the app store and RevenueCat, and nothing you write or record.

3. What stays on your device

Everything you create in the app is stored locally:

  • your habits, the days you checked them off and each habit's reminder settings;
  • journal pages (text, mood and input mode) and the excerpts you highlighted;
  • the photos and videos you add to the journal, with their captions;
  • your reminders and the reviews that were generated;
  • your first-use answers and preferences, such as language, theme and the daily reminder time.

Text, habits, reminders and captions live in an encrypted database (AES-256). The key is kept in the system's secure storage (Keychain on iOS, Keystore on Android) and never leaves the device. Photos and videos are stored as files in the app's private folder, outside that database: only the app can access them, and they get the protection the system gives each app's data, but they do not go through the database's AES-256 encryption.

4. Permissions the app asks for

  • Microphone and speech recognition: used only when you tap to speak. Transcription happens on the device itself. Audio is not recorded or stored; the app keeps only the text you confirm. If the language is not available offline on the device, dictation does not work, instead of sending your voice to the cloud.
  • Camera and photos (optional): used only when you choose to take a photo or video, or pick one from your library, for the journal. The app keeps a copy in its private folder and does not read the rest of your library.
  • Notifications: alerts for your habits and reminders are scheduled on the device itself. No server of ours takes part. On Android, the app also asks for the exact alarms permission so the alert sounds at the set time.
  • Calendar (optional): if you choose "Add to calendar", Rastro creates, in the device's calendar, the events for the habits and reminders you asked for, and removes them when you turn the option off or delete the item. Before creating an event, it looks at the calendar only to check whether an identical event (same title and time) already exists, to avoid duplicating it. It does not use the rest of your schedule for other purposes. If your calendar is synced with an account (iCloud, Google), that account keeps the events, under its own rules.
  • Internet: used for iCloud sync, when you turn it on, and for the Pro subscription (section 8). Rastro does not use the internet to send what you write to any server of ours.

You can deny or revoke any permission in the device's Settings. The app keeps working, without the corresponding feature.

5. iCloud sync (optional, iPhone only)

It is off until you turn it on. When on, Rastro stores your data in your private iCloud database (CloudKit), so your Apple devices stay identical. Text, habits, reminders and captions are sent with end-to-end encryption. We, as developers, have no access to this content. Apple uses its own notification service to tell your other devices that something changed. The storage counts against your iCloud quota and follows Apple's privacy policy.

Photos and videos are different: the files go to iCloud as CloudKit assets, which do not have end-to-end encryption. Apple protects them at rest and only your account can access them, but they do not have the same protection as the text. In the app's Settings you can turn off sending photos and videos to iCloud and keep everything else synced. There is no sync on Android.

6. Apple Watch and widgets

In versions of the app that include the Apple Watch, the iPhone sends the paired watch today's summary (habit names, which are done and the next time), through Apple's system. Widgets read, on the device itself, a summary of the day (habit progress and the next reminder). This content goes nowhere else.

7. Sharing

The review image is created on the device and handed to the system share sheet. You choose where it goes. It contains only the month, the opening line and the consistency rate; journal quotes, highlighted excerpts, photos and videos are not included. Rastro does not send the image anywhere. Likewise, if you open a journal photo or video and tap share, the system takes it wherever you choose.

8. Rastro Pro subscription and purchases

The Pro plan is sold by the App Store or Google Play. Payment is made and kept by them: we do not receive your name, email, card or other payment details.

To know whether you have an active subscription and to show the plans with their prices, the app uses RevenueCat (a third-party service, revenuecat.com/privacy). It receives an anonymous identifier that it generates for the app, purchase and subscription information (product, dates, status), the store's language and currency, and technical data inherent to any internet connection, such as the IP address. No habit, journal text, reminder, photo, video or review is sent to them. In the RevenueCat dashboard we see purchase history tied to that anonymous identifier, not to your identity. We do not use this data for advertising or tracking.

Rastro works on the free plan without any of these connections beyond the store lookup.

9. Third parties

Rastro includes no analytics, advertising or tracking SDKs. The only third-party service included in the app is RevenueCat, for the subscription (section 8). The system services it uses (iCloud, notifications, calendar, camera and photos, speech recognition, the app rating prompt and the app store) have their own policies, from Apple and Google. The app's fonts are bundled in it; the app does not download them from the internet.

10. How to erase your data

  • On the device: uninstalling the app erases the database, the photos and videos, and the encryption key. You can also delete each habit, page, photo or video inside the app.
  • In iCloud: Settings › your name › iCloud › Manage Storage › Rastro › Delete Data. This removes the data from iCloud, and the app turns sync off.
  • Calendar events: deleting the habit or turning off "Add to calendar" removes the event the app created.
  • Subscription: cancel it in the store settings (section 7 of the Terms of Use explains how). Purchase records stay with Apple or Google and, anonymously, with RevenueCat. To request deletion of the RevenueCat record, write to the contact below.

Since we do not keep the app's content, there is nothing to erase on our side.

11. Your rights (LGPD and similar laws)

Brazil's data protection law (LGPD), like similar laws elsewhere, grants rights such as access, correction, deletion and portability. Because the data is with you, you exercise those rights in the app and on your device: everything Rastro keeps, you can see, edit and delete there. For purchase data (section 8), and for any question or request, write to the contact below.

12. Children and teenagers

Rastro does not collect anyone's content, including that of children and teenagers: there is no account, sign-up, ads or sharing of information. Minors should use the app with a parent or guardian's knowledge, especially if they turn on iCloud sync, calendar access or a subscription.
